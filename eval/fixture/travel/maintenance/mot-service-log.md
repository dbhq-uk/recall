# MOT and service log

## The van

2016 Volkswagen Transporter T6 LWB panel van, 2.0 TDI 102PS, engine code CXHA, 6-speed manual, registration OU16 ZBP. Bought June 2022 from a dealer in Barnsley at 61,400 miles, specifically for the conversion — chose the LWB over the SWB for the extra length behind the rear axle, which made the difference between a permanent fixed bed and a fold-out arrangement.

## MOT history

### March 2024

Pass. One advisory: nearside rear tyre showing wear on the inner edge, consistent with tracking being slightly out. Booked an alignment check the following week — tracking was indeed off, likely from a pothole strike over winter, corrected under warranty at the fitter's cost since it had been done there originally.

### March 2025

Pass. One advisory: offside headlamp aim slightly high. Adjusted at home using the adjuster screw behind the unit rather than booking it in — five-minute job with the van against a wall and a tape measure.

## Service log

### Cambelt and water pump

Replaced at 78,000 miles, September 2023, at Hillfoot Motors in Sheffield. £540 including parts and labour. VW's interval for this engine is 5 years or 80,000 miles, whichever comes first — van was approaching both limits simultaneously so didn't want to push it, especially given how catastrophic a snapped belt is on an interference engine like this one. Water pump replaced at the same time as standard practice given the labour overlap, even though it wasn't showing any fault.

### Brakes

Front discs and pads replaced January 2025 at 92,300 miles. Went with Brembo aftermarket discs rather than VW originals — noticeably cheaper and reviews suggested equivalent quality for a vehicle that isn't being tracked or towing heavy loads regularly. Rears were still within tolerance at the same inspection, left alone.

### Oil

5W30 longlife oil, VW 507 00 spec. Service interval is 20,000 miles or 2 years, whichever comes first — the long-life oil spec is part of why the interval can stretch that far compared to a conventional 10,000 mile service.

#### Filter part number

Mann W712/94, kept a couple of spares in the garage since it's a common enough part to buy in bulk.

## Tyres

Matched set of four fitted May 2024: Continental VanContact 4Season, size 235/65 R16C. Went for the all-season compound specifically because of how much winter driving in Scotland the van does — swapping between summer and winter tyres twice a year wasn't practical given where it's parked.

## DPF notes

The diesel particulate filter has been the single most persistent niggle with this engine. Because a lot of our driving is short trips around Sheffield rather than sustained motorway runs, the DPF wasn't getting hot enough often enough to complete a passive regeneration cycle, and by autumn 2022 the dash was throwing up the DPF warning light roughly every couple of weeks. Took it to the dealer for a forced regeneration in November 2022, which involves plugging into the diagnostic system and running the engine at a controlled fast idle until the filter burns off the built-up soot — took about 25 minutes and cleared the light immediately.

Rather than keep paying for forced regens, changed driving habits instead: now make a point of doing at least one 25–30 minute continuous motorway or dual-carriageway run at a proper cruising RPM once a month if the van hasn't naturally had one, specifically to let a passive or active regen complete on its own. Since adopting that routine in early 2023 the warning light hasn't reappeared. Worth noting for anyone buying a diesel primarily for short urban journeys — the DPF problem is really a driving-pattern problem more than a mechanical one, and it's much cheaper to solve with a monthly motorway run than repeated trips to a dealer.
