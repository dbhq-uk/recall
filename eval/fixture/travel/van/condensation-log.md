# Condensation log

A running record of every damp check done on the van since the build finished,
kept because damp problems are slow and my memory of "was it like this last
winter?" is worthless. Readings taken with a cheap infrared thermometer and, since
2024, a £15 hygrometer velcroed to the bulkhead.

This is a log of observations, not a design document. The materials that went in
and the reasoning behind choosing them are written up in the insulation notes.
Here I am only recording what has actually happened since.

## Method

On a cold, still morning with two people having slept in the van overnight:

1. Read the hygrometer before opening anything. Anything above 70% relative
   humidity is a bad night.
2. Infrared thermometer on each panel in turn — offside wall, nearside wall,
   ceiling, both wheel arch boxes, floor, rear doors, both cab doors.
3. Anything reading more than about 3°C below its neighbours gets pulled and
   looked at, because a cold spot is a bridge and a bridge is where the water
   will end up.

## Log

### July 2022 — first check, one month after the build

Found a damp patch behind the rear nearside wheel arch trim. Traced, fixed,
written up properly in the insulation notes. Mentioned here only so the log is
complete from the start.

### January 2023 — first proper winter

Overnight low of -4°C in Sheffield, two of us in the van on the drive as a test.
Hygrometer read 74% on waking, which is high. Visible misting on the inside of
both cab door windows and nowhere else. Panel temperatures across the Thinsulate
sections were within 2°C of each other and of the ceiling, which is the result you
want. The cab doors were 6°C colder than anything in the living space.

Conclusion at the time: the living space is fine, the cab is not, and the cab was
always going to be the weak point since the door cards were left largely alone.
Bought a set of insulated cab window covers rather than tearing the door cards
apart.

### March 2023 — with the cab covers

Same test, similar conditions, -2°C overnight. Hygrometer 66%. No misting
anywhere. The covers were a £70 fix for a problem that would have cost a weekend
and a set of trim clips to solve properly, and they have stayed in the van ever
since.

### November 2023 — checking the wheel arch again

Specifically re-checked the rear nearside arch a year on from the repair. Dry,
same temperature as the surrounding wall, no smell. The extra Armaflex strip over
the missed rib join has held.

### February 2024 — first hygrometer-only check

Two nights on the NC500 route reruns, no infrared thermometer to hand. Peak
overnight reading 71%, dropping to 58% within about ten minutes of cracking the
roof fan open on its lowest setting. Worth noting how quickly the fan shifts it —
the temptation on a cold night is to seal everything up, and doing so is exactly
how you wake up with water running down a window.

### December 2024 and January 2025 — routine

Nothing to report. 62% and 68% respectively, no cold spots, no visible moisture.
Logged for the sake of the trend line.

### February 2026 — under the floor

Lifted a section of the ply subfloor for the first time since the build to run a
new cable, and took the chance to look underneath. The PIR board and the original
floor ridges were completely dry — no staining, no smell, no rust bloom on the
metal. This was the thing I had been quietly worried about for three years, since
water under a floor is invisible until the ply gives, and it turns out to have been
worry for its own sake.

## What the trend says

Six checks over four winters, and the pattern is boringly consistent: the living
space holds its temperature evenly and stays dry; the cab is cold and always will
be; and ventilation does more for the hygrometer reading than anything else in the
van. The single most effective thing anyone can do about condensation in a van is
open a vent, which is free, and the single least effective is to add another
centimetre of anything.
