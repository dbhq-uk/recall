# Water system

## Fresh water tank

40 litre semi-rigid tank (Waxtank) fitted under the rear bed frame, roughly central rather than tucked against an outer wall — deliberately kept away from both the exhaust run and the outer skin so it's slower to freeze and slower to cook in summer sun. 40L was a compromise: big enough for four or five days of careful use for two people, small enough that a full tank isn't adding an awkward 40kg right at the back axle.

## Pump

Shurflo 12V diaphragm pump, 3.5 GPM rated flow, pressure switch cuts in at 20psi and cuts out at 40psi.

### Mounting

#### Vibration fix

Mounted on rubber grommets to isolate the buzzing from the plywood cabinetry — the first fitting, direct to a wooden shelf, transmitted so much vibration noise it sounded like it was about to shake itself apart. The grommet kit came from a car audio supplier rather than a marine chandler, cheaper for the same job.

## Filtration

Inline carbon block filter between tank and pump, replaced roughly every six months. First replacement was January 2023 after the water started tasting faintly of plastic from the new tank — carbon filter cured it within a day of running water through.

## Taps

Reich telescopic swivel mixer tap at the sink, folds down flat when not in use to keep the worktop clear for cooking.

## Grey water

No fixed waste tank. Use a 20 litre collapsible container under the sink instead, emptied at proper waste points. Decided against a fixed tank for two reasons: weight (a full fixed tank at the same capacity would add dead weight low down that's awkward to drain on uneven ground), and because a lot of the wild camping we do isn't near a formal drain point anyway, so a portable container that can be carried to one is more useful than a tank that has to be driven to one.

## Freezing risk

Below about -3°C overnight the external fill hose freezes solid first. Now disconnect and drain it fully before any night forecast below zero, rather than relying on the tank's more sheltered position alone. The tank itself has never actually frozen solid in three winters, but the exposed hose length outside the insulated cabinetry has no thermal protection at all and is the weak point every time.

## Winterising checklist

Run through this before laying the van up for any length of time in cold weather:

```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# winterising checklist - water system
# run through in order, tick off as you go

# 1. drain the fresh water tank via the low-point drain valve
# 2. open all taps to release trapped pressure in the lines
# 3. run the pump briefly with taps open until it runs dry
#    (don't run it dry for more than a few seconds - diaphragm wear)
# 4. disconnect and drain the external fill hose separately
# 5. remove and store the carbon filter cartridge somewhere dry -
#    leaving it wet in situ over winter breeds mould in the housing
# 6. leave all taps cracked open so any residual moisture can't
#    freeze and split a fitting

echo "Water system winterised - checklist complete"
```

First winter (2022/23) we skipped step 4 and the external fill hose split at a joint after a hard frost in Sheffield in December — small job to replace but an annoying one to discover the following spring when it leaked all over the drive during the first refill.
