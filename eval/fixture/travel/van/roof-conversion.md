# Roof conversion

## Decision: raised roof vs pop-top

Went with a fixed GRP high-top roof rather than a pop-top. Considered a pop-top seriously for a while — the aerodynamic and stealth-camping advantages are real — but landed on the raised roof for three reasons: full standing headroom of 1.98m throughout rather than just in the pop-top section, no canvas walls to maintain or condensation-soak through in wet Scottish weather, and one less mechanical thing (lift struts, catches) to fail down the line. The stealth argument didn't weigh much for us since we're not trying to hide the van as a camper anyway.

## Fitting

Fitted by All Seasons Bodyshop in Rotherham, over three working days in July 2022. £2,850 supply and fit, which included the roof shell itself, structural bonding to the original roof skin, and making good the headlining trim around the new roof line. Chose a bodyshop that specialises in this over a general conversion company — wanted someone confident with GRP bonding and paint matching specifically, since a bad bond here is a leak waiting to happen for the life of the van.

## Roof vent and fan

MaxxFan Deluxe 4500K fitted with rain sensor and remote control, positioned over the kitchen area rather than dead centre of the roof, so steam and cooking smells vent directly rather than drifting over the bed first. Cut into the roof through a 400mm x 400mm aperture. The rain sensor closes the lid automatically if it starts raining while the fan's left running — useful more times than expected, including once when we'd gone into a shop in Ullapool and left it open.

## Solar panel mounting

The solar panel sits on Renogy tilt-mount brackets, but rather than the usual four self-tapping screws through the roof, bonded the brackets down with Sikaflex 291i marine adhesive instead. Reasoning: every screw through a GRP roof is a future leak point, and the roof is large enough and light enough that adhesive alone holds firmly without wind-lift concerns at motorway speed. Only actual penetration in the whole roof is the single cable gland for the solar wiring, sealed the same way as the vent aperture.

## No roof rack

Deliberately didn't fit a roof rack or roof bars. Extra unsprung weight up high changes the handling more than people expect on a van this size, and we don't carry anything that needs to go up there — bikes go on a tow-bar rack instead.

## Cutting the vent aperture

This was the single most nerve-wracking day of the whole conversion, because there's no undoing a hole cut in the wrong place. Marked out the 400mm square twice, checked clearance underneath against the kitchen unit position and the roof rib pattern, then checked it a third time the next morning before committing. Used a jigsaw with a fine metal-cutting blade for the first few centimetres through the GRP skin to establish a clean line, then switched to a larger blade once through, running slow to avoid cracking the gelcoat at the corners — went in from a pilot hole at each corner rather than trying to turn the jigsaw through a tight radius, which is where most horror stories about cracked roofs online seem to originate.

Once the aperture was cut, dry-fitted the MaxxFan frame to check the fit before any sealant went anywhere, since it's much easier to trim a slightly-tight aperture than to fill an oversized one. Sealed with a bed of Sikaflex 512 caulk under the frame flange, then a secondary bead around the outside edge once screwed down through the flange into the roof skin — belt and braces given how much this would cost to get wrong.

Leak-tested that evening with a garden hose run over the roof for a solid ten minutes at various angles while someone sat inside with a torch checking the frame edges from underneath. Found one small weep at the rear corner where the caulk bed had a thin spot — could see a slightly damp line on the felt-tip pen marks we'd drawn round the frame edge as a tell. Let it dry out for two days, then went back in with an extra bead of Sikaflex specifically at that corner, pushed well into the gap with a gloved finger rather than just laid on top. Re-tested with the hose, dry. Been through three winters since with no sign of a leak.

A couple of things I'd do differently. Buy the sealant in bigger cartridges from the start — went through three 300ml tubes of Sikaflex 512 across the frame and the follow-up fix, and the last one ran out mid-bead, meaning a short delay driving to get another while the first section started skinning over. And mask up more generously before cutting — GRP dust from the jigsaw gets everywhere and took longer to clean off the roof paint than expected. The whole vent installation, fan unit included, came to a bit over £640 all in, separate from the £2,850 roof-fitting cost above.
