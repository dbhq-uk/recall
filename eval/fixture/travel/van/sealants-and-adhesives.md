# Sealants and adhesives — what goes where

A reference sheet, written after buying the wrong tube three times. This lists
what each product in the cupboard is actually for. It does not re-argue any of
the build decisions — where a decision was made about bonding versus mechanical
fixing, the reasoning is written up in the relevant build note and this sheet just
points at it.

Everything below is what is currently in the van cupboard or the garage shelf.

## The Sikaflex family

Sika make a confusing number of products with adjacent numbers and no obvious
naming logic, so:

### Sikaflex 291i

Marine-grade polyurethane adhesive sealant. Structural: it bonds as well as
seals, and it stays flexible enough to cope with a steel or GRP panel expanding
in the sun and contracting overnight. This is the one used for the solar panel
bracket bonding on the roof — see the roof conversion notes for how that was
decided and why. Skins in around an hour, full cure in 24–48 hours depending on
humidity. Once it has gone off, it is not coming off without a multitool and a bad
afternoon.

### Sikaflex 512 Caravan

The caulking one. Not structural — it is a sealant, not an adhesive, whatever the
tube implies. Used under the MaxxFan frame flange and around the vent aperture,
and for the cable gland. Paintable, sands badly, stays flexible.

### Sikaflex 252

Higher-strength structural adhesive, black. Bought for the awning rail and never
used for anything else. Overkill for most van jobs.

### Sikaflex 221

The general-purpose one that the internet recommends for everything. Fine, but
there is almost no job on this van where either 291i or 512 is not a better
answer, and having a fourth tube open just means three of them skin over before
they are finished.

## Everything else

### Butyl tape

Non-curing, stays tacky forever. Used behind window frames and under the flare
trims, where the point is to keep water out of a mechanical joint rather than to
hold anything together. It will never fully set, which is a feature — the joint
can be broken and re-made without destroying the panel.

### Captain Tolley's Creeping Crack Cure

A watery, capillary-action sealant for a leak you cannot find. Dribble it along
the suspect seam, let capillary action pull it into whatever hairline gap is
letting water in, repeat over several days. Bought after a persistent damp patch
that turned out to be a windscreen seam rather than anything on the roof at all.
It works, but it is a diagnosis-avoidance tool: if you know where the leak is, fix
the leak properly.

### Armaflex 520 contact adhesive

For sticking closed-cell insulation to metal. Not a sealant, does not belong in
this cupboard, keeps ending up in it anyway.

### Sikaflex remover / Sika Aktivator-205

The activator is a wipe-on primer for glass and painted surfaces before bonding.
Skipping it is the most common reason a bond fails, and it is the step everyone
skips, including me, once.

## Rules of thumb

- **Sealing a mechanical joint that will be screwed down anyway:** 512 or butyl.
- **Bonding something that has no mechanical fixing at all:** 291i, and degrease
  and activate the surface first, properly.
- **Anything that has to survive a jet wash:** not butyl.
- **Anything you might want to remove one day:** not 291i.

## Cartridge count from the build

Recorded for anyone estimating quantities, because every guide underestimates:
three 300ml tubes of 512 across the roof vent frame and the follow-up fix, one and
a half tubes of 291i across the solar brackets and the awning rail, and most of a
roll of butyl behind the two side windows. Buy the bigger cartridges from the
start.
