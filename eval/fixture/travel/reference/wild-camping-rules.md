# Wild camping rules — quick reference

Notes to myself so I stop having to look this up every trip. Not legal advice, just what I've established from official sources for each country we've actually camped in.

## Scotland

Wild camping (and by extension, overnighting in a van in a similar spirit — parking rather than pitching a tent, but the same courtesy principles apply) is broadly protected under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, which grants statutory access rights to most land and inland water for recreational purposes, provided it's exercised responsibly under the Scottish Outdoor Access Code — leave no trace, keep well away from houses, don't camp in enclosed fields with livestock or crops.

### Exception: East Loch Lomond

Byelaws introduced around the east shore of Loch Lomond restrict camping without a permit during the main season (roughly March to September) within a defined management zone, following years of problems with litter and antisocial behaviour at informal lakeside spots. Worth checking the specific zone boundary before assuming the general Scottish access right applies there.

## England and Wales

No general statutory right to wild camp — technically requires the landowner's permission, unlike Scotland. Dartmoor was the long-standing exception, where camping had been permitted under specific commons legislation for decades, until a 2023 legal challenge (Darwall v Dartmoor National Park Authority) found that the relevant byelaws didn't actually grant a right to camp without permission after all. That was contested and partly reversed on appeal later in 2023, restoring some backpack camping rights on Dartmoor commons, but the whole episode is a reminder that England's position is fragile and locally specific rather than a blanket right — always worth checking current status for Dartmoor specifically rather than assuming the old rule of thumb still holds everywhere.

## France

Sleeping overnight in a motorhome or van on an ordinary public road or car park is generally tolerated across France unless there's a specific sign prohibiting it (often phrased as "camping interdit" or showing a tent/caravan symbol with a line through it) — this is different from "wild camping" in the tent sense, which is more locally restricted, particularly within national parks. The aires network and the France Passion scheme (see the France trip log for specific sites used) make this largely unnecessary anyway outside of remote areas.

## Spain

More restrictive in practice than the France, even though the letter of the law is broadly similar (overnighting tolerated, camping — awnings out, chairs unfolded, clearly settling in — generally not, without a specific site). Enforcement by the Guardia Civil varies a lot regionally. The Balearic Islands are the strictest example we've come across directly: overnight parking of motorhomes is banned outright in a lot of coastal and protected areas, with fines that get mentioned often enough in van forums to take seriously rather than risk it.

## Germany

Similar to France in spirit — overnighting generally fine on ordinary parking, actual camping behaviour is not, without permission.
