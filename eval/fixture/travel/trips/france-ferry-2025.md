# France, April 2025

## Ferry booking

Brittany Ferries, Portsmouth to Caen, overnight crossing on the Mont St Michel, sailed 5 April 2025. Booked a two-berth cabin rather than a reclining seat — worth the extra for an overnight crossing, arrive at Ouistreham properly rested rather than having been awake in a lounge all night. £268 return for the van plus two passengers and the cabin, booked about eight weeks ahead, which seemed to get a noticeably better rate than looking a fortnight out for the same dates when we compared.

## Route through Normandy and the Loire

First few days around the Normandy landing beaches — Arromanches, the American cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer, Pointe du Hoc — before heading south through the Loire valley towards Chinon and Amboise, then back up towards the coast for the return sailing from Caen on 19 April.

### Aires used

France makes this kind of trip genuinely easy compared to the UK. Municipal aires (dedicated motorhome service and overnight stopover points, usually with water, waste disposal and sometimes electric hook-up for a few euros) were available in nearly every town we passed through:

- Bayeux municipal aire, walking distance to the cathedral, €8/night including electricity.
- Aire at Amboise right on the Loire, view of the château from the van, €10/night.
- Small free aire at a village near Chinon whose name I didn't note down properly — just a gravel area behind the mairie with a water point, no charge at all.

Also stayed twice through the France Passion network — a scheme where farms, vineyards and small producers let motorhomes stay overnight for free in exchange for visiting their shop or at least the goodwill of supporting a small producer. Stopped at a cider producer near Cambremer this way; bought a case in the morning before leaving, felt like a fair exchange on both sides. Needed the annual France Passion guidebook (bought online before the trip) to find the sites, since they're not signposted or on the general aires apps in the same way municipal ones are.

## Costs

Tolls on the péage network from Caen down towards the Loire and back came to about €54 total across the fortnight — French autoroutes are toll roads by default unlike most UK roads, easy to forget when route-planning. Diesel was noticeably cheaper than at home, supermarket forecourts (Leclerc, Intermarché) consistently a few cents a litre below the motorway service stations, worth the small detour to fill up off the autoroute rather than at the péage services.

## Week two itinerary

The second week, once we'd looped down to the Loire, settled into a slower rhythm than the first — less driving, more staying two nights in one place rather than moving on daily. Chinon was the highlight: parked at a small aire a short walk from the old town, spent a full day just wandering the fortress and the river frontage without the van moving at all, first time on the trip we'd properly switched off from route-planning. Amboise the next stop was busier with tourist traffic than expected for April, but the aire's position right on the Loire more than made up for it — sat outside with the château lit up in the evening, about as good a "free" view as we've had anywhere.

Weather across the second week was consistently better than Normandy had been — dry, bright, low teens most days, ideal for sitting out with the awning up rather than being confined to the van itself. Picked up a slow puncture somewhere around Amboise, noticed the tyre pressure warning light on the dash rather than anything visibly wrong; limped to a village garage the next morning who found a small screw and repaired it on the spot for €15, no drama. Spent the last two days working back north in shorter hops, deliberately building in slack before the return sailing rather than risking a tight connection at Ouistreham — a lesson from a previous trip where a missed toll junction turned what should have been a comfortable margin into a genuinely rushed final hour to the port.

Overall the aires-and-France-Passion approach worked out both cheaper and more interesting than sticking to campsites the whole way — total accommodation cost for the fortnight, ferry aside, came to under €90, and several of the best evenings of the trip were the free ones.

The final push back to Ouistreham was unhurried given the lesson mentioned above — two easy days, stopping overnight at the Bayeux aire again, arriving a comfortable two and a half hours before boarding. The return sailing was noticeably rougher, a stiff crosswind that had the stabilisers working hard, though nothing that caused real trouble. Docked into Portsmouth after 7am, home in Sheffield by early afternoon. Best decision of the fortnight was building in that slack rather than treating the sailing as a hard deadline.
