# Reading Notes

Book notes and a running to-read list. Ratings out of 5, mostly for my own future reference on whether to recommend something.

## Finished

*Fiction:*

**Piranesi** by Susanna Clarke, finished January 2024, 5/5. Read it in three sittings over a weekend, which is rare for me these days. The house-as-world conceit could have been gimmicky but the slow reveal of what's actually happening, and who the narrator really is, is handled with real restraint. Nothing in the prose is wasted — worth a reread purely to catch the clues seeded in the first fifty pages that only make sense on a second pass.

**The Overstory** by Richard Powers, finished March 2024, 4/5. Structurally ambitious — nine character strands that slowly converge — and the writing about trees themselves is genuinely beautiful in places. Docked a point because the back third feels like it loses some momentum once the activist plotline takes over; the quieter character studies in the first half were what hooked me.

**Klara and the Sun** by Kazuo Ishiguro, finished May 2024, 4/5. Told from the perspective of an artificial companion, and the restrained, slightly-off narration voice is the whole trick of the book — it takes a while to trust what Klara is telling you versus what's actually happening around her. Quietly devastating ending without ever raising its voice.

*Non-fiction:*

**Four Thousand Weeks** by Oliver Burkeman, finished February 2024, 5/5. A book about time management that spends most of its energy arguing you should give up on time management as usually understood. The chapter on "cosmic insignificance therapy" has stuck with me more than any productivity system I've tried and abandoned over the years.

**Braiding Sweetgrass** by Robin Wall Kimmerer, finished June 2024, 4/5. Essays blending botany, indigenous knowledge, and memoir. Some essays landed much harder than others — the one on the Three Sisters planting method (corn, beans, squash grown together) was a genuine "why did nobody tell me this before" moment, relevant enough that it's changed how I think about companion planting generally, even if the specifics don't map neatly onto what grows well here.

**Sapiens** by Yuval Noah Harari, finished 2023 (before this list started properly, so no exact date), 3/5. Interesting in places, overconfident in others — some of the anthropology claims read as more settled than they actually are in the field. Good for provoking dinner-table arguments, less good as a reliable single source.

## Currently reading

**The Left Hand of Darkness** by Ursula K. Le Guin, started this week. Early impressions: the world-building around Gethenian ambisexuality is doing a lot of quiet philosophical work without ever stopping to lecture about it, which is the mark of someone who trusts the reader.
