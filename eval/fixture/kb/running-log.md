# Running Log

Training notes since taking up running again in early 2023 after a long gap. Mostly road and towpath, one half marathon under the belt so far.

## Kit

### Shoes

- **Saucony Endorphin Speed 3** — main training and race shoe, nylon plate rather than full carbon, bought after a gait analysis at a local running shop suggested something with a bit more structure than my previous neutral trainers.
- **Asics Gel-Kayano 29** — stability shoe, bought specifically during a bout of shin splints in mid-2023 on physio advice, now used for easy recovery runs and anything over 8km on tired legs.

### Watch

Garmin Forerunner 255, worn for every run, mainly for pace and heart rate rather than the more advanced training load metrics which I don't fully trust yet.

## Heart rate zones

Set these using a lab-estimated max HR of 189bpm rather than the age-based formula, which felt too generous when tested against how hard efforts actually felt:

- Zone 2 (easy/aerobic): 132-151bpm
- Zone 3 (tempo): 152-165bpm
- Zone 4 (threshold): 166-176bpm
- Zone 5 (max effort): 177bpm+

Most weekday runs are meant to stay in zone 2, which took real discipline to accept — the pace feels almost embarrassingly slow at first, walk-adjacent on hills.

## Routes

### Canal towpath loop

Standard weekday route: out along the canal towpath, under the ring road bridge, turning at the second lock gate, back the same way. Comes out at almost exactly 8km door to door, flat except for a short ramp up to street level at each end. This is the default easy-run route simply because it's flat and traffic-free, which makes it easier to actually hold zone 2 pace.

### Long run route

Sunday long runs head out through the park, up past the reservoir, and loop back via the old railway path — around 16km depending on which reservoir path is taken (the lower path is shorter but gets muddy after rain).

## Parkrun

Fartown parkrun most Saturdays when not doing a long run that day. Personal best 22:41, set in September 2023 on a cool, still morning — conditions matter more there than people admit, a windy week can add 40-plus seconds for the same effort.

## Injury: shin splints, summer 2023

Medial tibial stress syndrome, diagnosed by a physio in June 2023 after about three weeks of a dull ache along the inside of both shins that got worse during runs rather than better. Contributing factors identified: ramped weekly mileage too quickly (went from roughly 20km/week to 35km/week over three weeks, well beyond the usual 10% guidance), old shoes with worn-out midsoles, and running the same flat towpath route every single day without variation, which the physio reckoned meant the same muscles were being loaded identically run after run with no recovery pattern.

Treatment was frustratingly unglamorous: complete rest from running for two weeks, calf raises and tibialis anterior strengthening exercises daily, and a gradual walk-run reintroduction over a further three weeks. The Gel-Kayano purchase came out of that appointment — more support through the midfoot, on the physio's recommendation, for the higher-mileage weeks going forward. Also started alternating the towpath route with the park loop on back-to-back easy days, purely to vary the camber and surface rather than for any real training-load reason.

Fully resolved by August 2023, no recurrence since, though the calf and tibialis exercises are still done twice a week as a maintenance habit rather than something to drop once symptom-free.

## Half marathon — Riverside Half, March 2024

Twelve-week training block, peak week around 55km including one long run of 18km. Race day conditions were good — cool, dry, minimal wind. Went out at planned pace through halfway (1:44 on the watch at 10.5km) but faded over the last 4km as the legs hadn't done a long run past 18km in training, finishing in 2:21:07. Slower than the 2:15 goal but a solid first half marathon result, and a clear note for next time: at least one 20km-plus long run before race day, not 18km as the ceiling.
