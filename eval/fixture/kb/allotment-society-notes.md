# Briar Lane Allotment Society — notes

Notes from the site AGMs and the committee emails, kept because things get decided
at these meetings that affect the plot and nobody writes them down anywhere
findable afterwards. Site-wide matters only; anything specific to what I am
actually growing is in the plot notes.

## The site

Sixty-two plots, roughly half of them full-size, the rest split into halves after
the waiting list hit four years in 2019. Council-owned, managed by the society
under a lease. Standpipes at three points, no electricity, no vehicular access
past the gate except for deliveries.

## Subscriptions

- 2024: £42 a year for a full plot, £26 for a half.
- 2025: £45 / £28.
- 2026: £45 / £28, held after a reasonably heated discussion at the AGM.

The 2026 freeze was pushed through against the committee's own recommendation. The
argument that carried the room was that the water bill had gone up because of a
leaking standpipe nobody had fixed for eight months, and that plot-holders should
not be paying for that twice.

## Blight watch

The society runs a blight alert on the site WhatsApp group, triggered whenever the
Hutton Criteria are met at the nearest weather station — two consecutive days with
a minimum temperature above 10°C and at least six hours of relative humidity above
90%. When an alert goes out, the advice is the same every time: check your
foliage, remove and bag anything with lesions, do not compost it.

### 2023

The wet August of 2023 was, by common agreement at that year's AGM, the worst
blight year anyone on the site could remember. Alerts went out on the 4th, 9th and
11th of August and then more or less continuously for the rest of the month. The
damage across the site was extremely uneven — some plots lost everything within a
fortnight, others came through almost untouched, and the difference seemed to come
down to variety choice and how quickly people acted rather than anything about
where a plot sat on the site.

Several plot-holders said afterwards they were switching to resistant maincrop
varieties, Sarpo Mira being the one named most often in the discussion. Nobody
recorded any actual figures, which is a shame, because the AGM spent forty minutes
on anecdote and could have spent five on data if anyone had thought to collect it.
The committee floated a shared blight-outcome log for future years. It was agreed
enthusiastically and has never been mentioned since.

### 2024 and 2025

Two alerts in 2024, none at all in 2025. Both quiet years. The 2025 AGM lasted
under an hour, which the chair described as the best blight season of his life.

## Other business, in brief

- **Shed break-ins, winter 2024.** Four sheds over two months, tools taken from
  two. The society bought a padlock bulk order and split the cost; the police
  logged it and, predictably, nothing came of it.
- **Bonfire policy.** Permitted November to February only, after 4pm, and not at
  all if the wind is toward the houses on the lane. This has been re-litigated at
  every single AGM since 2021 and the policy has never once changed.
- **Manure delivery.** A farm near Ecclesfield drops well-rotted horse manure in
  the car park each October; £3 a barrow, honesty box. Reliably the most popular
  thing the society does all year.
- **The waiting list.** Down to about two and a half years, from four. Committee
  attributes this to the half-plot split; a cynic would attribute it to how much
  harder people found the 2023 season.

## The one useful thing I have taken from these meetings

The Hutton Criteria alerts genuinely are worth paying attention to. Everything
else is politics about bonfires.
