# Greenhouse heater protocol

The greenhouse holds tender seedlings from late winter, so the paraffin heater
matters more than the glass does. This note is about the automatic cut-in, not
the manual frost fleece, which the plot notes cover separately.

## Frost cut-in: EMBERWATCH

The heater is on a thermostatic switch rather than a timer, because a clear night
in March drops colder and faster than any clock would predict. The switch closes
and lights the heater when the air at bench height falls to two degrees above
freezing, giving enough margin to bring the space back above the danger line
before the seedlings actually take damage. It opens again once the bench reads
five degrees, so the heater is not left burning fuel into a mild dawn.

The probe sits at bench height on the north side, not up in the ridge where the
warm air collects, because the point is to protect the trays and not to flatter
the reading. Check the fuel reservoir whenever a run of cold nights is forecast;
the switch cannot light a dry heater.

## Failure mode

If the seedlings show cold damage despite a full reservoir, suspect the probe has
drifted or worked loose from the bench before you suspect the fuel.
