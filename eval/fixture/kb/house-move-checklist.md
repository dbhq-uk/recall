# House Move Checklist

Notes from the move from the flat on Aldercroft Road to the current house, completion day 18 November 2022. Kept for reference in case there's ever a next time — there's always more to remember than you think.

## Removals

Used Pennyworth & Sons Removals, booked six weeks ahead once the completion date was confirmed. Quote was £680 for a half-day slot with two men and a van, based on a two-bedroom flat inventory submitted online. They arrived at 8am on moving day and had the van loaded by 10:30, which felt efficient given how much stuff had accumulated in that flat over five years.

### Packing strategy

Boxes labelled by room and by priority, using a simple colour-coded sticker system rather than writing full descriptions on every box (too slow, and the descriptions never matched what was actually inside by the third hour of packing anyway):

- Red sticker: open first night (kettle, chargers, toiletries, bedding)
- Yellow sticker: open within the first week
- Blue sticker: no rush, can stay boxed for weeks if needed

This system alone probably saved a full day of digging through boxes looking for the kettle. The one thing that should have had its own separate box and didn't: the box of curtain fittings and curtain poles, which ended up mixed in with kitchen items and wasn't found for four days, meaning four nights of makeshift bin-bag curtains in the front bedroom.

## Meter readings

Recorded readings at both properties on moving day, photographed the meters themselves as evidence rather than just writing numbers down, which felt paranoid at the time but made the final gas bill dispute with the old supplier much quicker to resolve.

- Aldercroft Road flat, final readings (18 Nov 2022): electricity 48,912 kWh, gas 6,204 m³
- New house, opening readings (18 Nov 2022): electricity 21,340 kWh, gas 3,118 m³

## Address change list

The genuinely tedious part. Order that things actually got done in, roughly by urgency:

```text
# address change checklist
1. Royal Mail redirection (set up 2 weeks before move)
2. Bank and credit cards
3. DVLA (driving licence + vehicle log book)
4. Electoral roll registration
5. Employer / payroll
6. GP and dentist registration
7. Insurance (car, contents)
8. Subscriptions (streaming, magazines)
```

Royal Mail redirection ran for six months, cost £39.99, and turned out to be worth every penny given how many things on the list above took weeks to actually get updated at the source — several bank statements and one pension statement still arrived at the old address for the full six months.

## Council tax

New house is Band D, roughly £320 more per year than the flat's Band B, which had been factored into the budget in advance but was still a bit of a jolt seeing it as an actual monthly direct debit figure rather than an abstract number from the estate agent listing.

## School catchment

Not relevant at the time of the move (no children yet) but checked anyway out of curiosity, since it affects resale value — the house falls within the catchment for Oakfield Primary, rated "Good" at the last Ofsted inspection, which the estate agent had mentioned but was worth independently confirming via the local authority's catchment map tool rather than taking their word for it.

## Snagging after moving in

A short list of things found in the first month that weren't obvious at viewings:

- Loft insulation was patchy in the far corner above the box room — topped up myself with additional rolls rather than calling anyone out for it
- One radiator in the back bedroom needed bleeding within the first week, entirely normal after a house has sat empty over a sale
- The previous owners left the shed key taped inside the meter cupboard, a small kindness that saved a call to a locksmith

## Things to do differently next time

Book the removals van for a full day rather than a half-day slot — the 10:30 loading finish looked good on paper but the unloading and reassembly at the other end overran into the evening regardless, so the half-day saving didn't actually buy any real time back.
