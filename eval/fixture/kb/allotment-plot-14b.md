# Allotment — Plot 14b

Notes on Plot 14b at Briar Lane allotments, taken on in November 2021. Roughly 125 square metres, south-facing, reasonable clay-loam soil once the builder's rubble near the gate was cleared out.

## Layout

Split into four rough beds plus a fruit corner. Shed at the top (secondhand, 6x4ft, needs a new felt roof at some point), water butt (200L, fed off the shed roof guttering) beside it, and a compost bay made from old pallets against the north fence.

## Seed varieties grown

### Tomatoes

- **Sungold** — cherry tomato, consistently the best cropper on the plot, sweet enough that they rarely make it back to the kitchen. Grown as cordons, four plants, up canes in the greenhouse-less open bed with just canvas fleece on cold nights early on.
- **Crimson Crush** — bred for blight resistance, beefsteak-type fruit. Genuinely held up during the wet August of 2023 when neighbouring plots lost most of their outdoor tomatoes to blight within about ten days.

### Alliums and roots

- **Musselburgh** leeks — sown early April, transplanted into dibbed holes in June once pencil-thick, earthed up through fungal collar in September. Reliable, hardy through frost, lifted as needed right through winter.
- **Tender and True** parsnip — long, smooth roots, low core, sown direct in April (parsnip seed doesn't transplant well) and thinned to 15cm spacing.
- **Boltardy** beetroot — bolt-resistant, sown in succession every three weeks from April to July for a continuous supply rather than one big glut.

### Potatoes

Two varieties each year:

- **Charlotte** — second early, waxy, planted late March, chitted on the kitchen windowsill beforehand for about four weeks.
- **Sarpo Mira** — maincrop, strong blight resistance (much better than Charlotte in that respect), planted mid-April, left in the ground until October.

## Sowing calendar

Roughly what gets sown when, kept as a rough guide rather than gospel since weather shifts things year to year:

```text
# Plot 14b sowing calendar (rough guide)
Mar   chit potatoes indoors, sow parsnip direct
Apr   plant potatoes, sow leeks, first beetroot sowing
May   plant out tomatoes (after last frost risk), sow beans
Jun   transplant leeks, second/third beetroot sowings
Jul   final beetroot sowing, harvest early potatoes
Sep   earth up leeks, harvest maincrop
Oct   lift Sarpo Mira, clear beds
```

## Pests

Slugs are the main enemy, particularly on the beetroot seedlings in a wet spring. Wool pellets around vulnerable rows help a little; nothing has worked completely.

## Blight, 2023

The wet August properly tested the tomato and potato varieties chosen partly for resistance. Charlotte potatoes, lifted well before blight typically takes hold, were unaffected as usual — early varieties mostly dodge it by being out of the ground already. The real test was Sarpo Mira against blight pressure that took out an untreated maincrop variety two plots over within about a week and a half of the first spots appearing on their foliage. Sarpo Mira showed a few leaf lesions by late August but the tubers themselves came up clean at lifting in October, no sign of the disease having reached below ground, which is exactly the trade-off that variety is bred for — tolerate some foliar damage but keep the crop.

The Crimson Crush tomatoes held on almost to the first frost with barely a mark, while a couple of self-sown "volunteer" tomato plants near the compost bay (presumably a stray Sungold seed from the previous year) collapsed to blight within days, going from healthy to blackened stems in what felt like no time at all. That contrast alone was reason enough to keep buying resistant varieties even though the seed costs more than a bog-standard tomato pack.

## Companion planting

Marigolds along the tomato bed edge, more for the colour and a vague hope of deterring whitefly than any rigorously proven effect. Nasturtiums left to sprawl near the beans as a sacrificial crop for blackfly, which does seem to draw some pressure away from the beans themselves.

## To try next year

Purple sprouting broccoli — never grown brassicas here yet, plot has no netting cage so cabbage white butterflies would likely be an issue. Might need to invest in mesh before attempting it.
