# Q3 2026 Planning and Marketing Review

## Revenue target

£185,000 for the quarter (July–September 2026), up from £164,000 actual in Q2. The increase is mostly the Northgate renewal landing cleanly and the first two Kestrel Launchpad engagements closing in Q2 and billing across Q3 — one of them to a returning client on the 5% loyalty discount, which is the first time that discount has actually been used since it was introduced.

## Marketing experiments

### LinkedIn ads — failure

We ran a six-week LinkedIn ads campaign in May–June 2026, £2,400 spent, targeting operations directors at logistics and healthcare SMEs across the South West — a fairly narrow audience chosen to mirror our two best existing client relationships. Result: three leads, zero conversions, and of the three leads only one was even a plausible fit for what we do. We've cut the budget rather than iterate on targeting; the honest read is that cold outbound to a role like "operations director" doesn't suit how we actually win work, which has always been referral and word-of-mouth led.

### Conference sponsorship — success

By contrast, sponsoring a stand at the West of England Tech Summit in October 2024 converted one paying client (see the client notes for which one and how). We repeated the sponsorship in October 2025 and are budgeting for October 2026 already as part of this quarter's plan — roughly £3,200 this time, up slightly from the £3,000 spent in each of the previous two years. Unlike the LinkedIn experiment, this one earns its place by putting us in front of exactly the kind of founder who's making a buying decision within the next six months, in a setting where a real conversation happens rather than a scroll-past.

## What we're not doing

We looked at and rejected a cold-email campaign (bought list, personalised at scale) on the basis that it would take more of Alex's time to run properly than the LinkedIn experiment did, for a channel we have even less confidence in. Parking it, not ruling it out permanently.

## Kestrel Launchpad adoption

Two Launchpad engagements sold in Q2 2026, both delivered within the six-week window with no scope changes — early evidence that the tightly defined offering does what it was designed to do, which is remove the discovery-and-negotiation overhead that eats margin on small fixed-price jobs. Too early to call it a trend off two data points, but worth tracking as its own line in next quarter's numbers rather than folding it into general project revenue.

## Headcount

Provisionally planning a mid-level developer hire for Q4 2026, contingent on Q3 landing at or above the £185,000 target. Not committing budget yet — this is a "if the number holds, we start the process in October" note, not a decision.

## Cost lines landing in Q3

Three known cost events sit inside the quarter and are already in the £185,000 plan:

- **Insurance renewal, 1 September.** The professional indemnity policy (HPI-3391847) and the cyber policy renew together through Cavendish. Budgeted at £2,350 combined on the assumption of another modest rate rise; Cavendish have not quoted yet. Cover levels are unchanged and are documented in the insurance notes, not here.
- **Conference sponsorship, October.** £3,200, committed. Falls just outside the quarter for cash but is invoiced within it.
- **VAT.** Second full year on standard accounting since leaving the Flat Rate Scheme, which now smooths across the quarter rather than landing as a single lump — the practical effect on quarterly cash forecasting is that the number is duller and more predictable than it used to be, which is exactly what we wanted.

## A note on how the target is built

The £185,000 is not a buffered number. Revenue targets are set from signed contracts plus a weighted pipeline, with no multiplier applied. The 1.4x risk buffer people sometimes ask about is a *pricing* device applied to fixed-price quotes, and it has nothing whatsoever to do with how we forecast a quarter — applying it here would mean quietly forecasting a number we do not believe. The rate card explains what the buffer is for; do not import it into this document.

## Risks to the target

The single biggest risk to hitting £185,000 is the Aldermoor Phase 2 scoping conversation slipping past their board budget approval into Q4 — if that engagement doesn't convert to a signed contract by the end of August, we're relying on Launchpad sales and a currently-unsigned prospect (a logistics referral through Northgate, still in early conversation) to close the gap. Worth flagging now rather than discovering it in September.
