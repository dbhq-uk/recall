# Business Banking — Switch to Starling Bank

## Background

We opened our original business account with Tide back at incorporation in 2019, when a fully online sign-up with no branch visit was still novel enough to feel like an obvious win over the high street banks we'd used personally. It served us fine for years while it was just Alex and Sam managing the account directly.

## Why we moved

### Multi-user permissions

Tide's permission model didn't let us give Ellie — our part-time bookkeeper — read access and payment-approval rights without also giving her full admin rights over the account, including the ability to add or remove other users and change account settings. That's more access than a two-day-a-week bookkeeping role should carry, and we didn't want to be the reason a small business banking story ends up in someone's "why you shouldn't do this" post.

### Xero feed reliability

The Tide-to-Xero bank feed dropped twice in 2025 — once in March, once in September — requiring a manual CSV re-import each time to catch up the reconciliation. Each incident cost Ellie roughly two weeks of delayed reconciliation before the backlog cleared, which doesn't sound like much until you're trying to produce management accounts for a quarter where half the transactions aren't matched yet.

## The switch

We moved to Starling Bank via the Current Account Switch Service (CASS), which completed on 11 February 2026. CASS moves all incoming payments, direct debits and standing orders automatically within a guaranteed seven working days, redirecting anything sent to the old account for a further three years — which mattered to us, since a couple of long-standing clients still had the old sort code and account number saved in their supplier records and we didn't want to be chasing payments that bounced.

Starling's permission model lets us set Ellie up with a role that can view all transactions and approve payments up to a threshold we set, without giving her the ability to add users or change account settings — exactly the gap Tide couldn't close.

## What we kept

Kept the same registered office and correspondence address throughout — the switch was purely the bank, nothing regulatory needed updating alongside it, and the Xero feed reconnected within a day of the new account going live.

## Outstanding follow-up

One direct debit — the AWS Business Support subscription, billed in USD — didn't transfer automatically under CASS because it's a foreign-currency arrangement outside the guarantee's scope, and had to be re-added manually against the new account details in March 2026. Worth remembering for anyone doing this again: check foreign-currency direct debits separately before assuming CASS has covered everything.

## Interest and fees

Starling's business current account pays a modest variable interest rate on the balance and charges no monthly fee, versus Tide's tiered plan which we'd been paying £9.99 a month for on the strength of features (extra cardholders, accounting integrations) that Starling includes for free. Not the primary reason for the switch, but a welcome side effect once Ellie totted it up: something under £120 a year saved on fees alone, which barely moves the needle next to the reconciliation time saved, but every little helps when you're justifying a change to the accountant.

## Would we do it again

Yes, without hesitation, and the main regret is not doing it sooner — we'd been grumbling about the Tide permissions model for the best part of a year before actually making the switch, mostly because "our bank account works, technically" kept losing out to more urgent client work on the priority list. Worth remembering next time something is merely annoying rather than broken: annoying compounds.
