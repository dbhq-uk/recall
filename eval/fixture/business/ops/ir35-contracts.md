# IR35 and Contractor Status

## Why this matters

Since the off-payroll working rules extended to private-sector medium and large clients in April 2021, the responsibility for determining IR35 status can sit with the end client rather than with us or with the contractor's own limited company. The statutory basis is Chapter 10, Part 2 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003). Getting this wrong exposes whoever is deemed the "fee-payer" to PAYE and National Insurance liabilities after the fact, which is not a mistake we want to make twice.

## Our associates

### Tom Vickers (Vickers Dev Ltd)

Tom has worked with us on and off since 2022, engaged through his own limited company, Vickers Dev Ltd. He's currently split across two engagements with materially different IR35 outcomes, which is a useful illustration of why status is assessed per engagement and not per person.

## Determinations by engagement

### Northgate — outside IR35

We are the fee-payer on the Northgate retainer (Northgate itself is a small business and falls under the pre-2021 rules where the old-style intermediaries legislation still applies, with the contractor's own company responsible for self-assessment, but we run the same CEST process regardless as good practice). Tom's Status Determination Statement was completed 3 October 2024, the same day the Northgate contract was signed, and came back outside IR35.

The reasoning: Tom exercised a genuine right of substitution in practice, not just on paper — he sent a substitute developer to cover a two-week gap in 2025 while he was on leave, and Northgate accepted the substitute without needing to approve the individual first. There's no mutuality of obligation beyond the agreed statement of work; Northgate isn't obliged to offer further work and Tom isn't obliged to accept it. He uses his own equipment and sets his own hours around agreed milestones rather than working to Northgate's timetable.

### Aldermoor — inside IR35

Aldermoor is a medium-sized business under the Companies Act size thresholds and therefore responsible for making its own status determinations under Chapter 10. Their SDS for Tom's work on the Phase 1 referral system, issued when he joined that workstream in August 2025, came back inside IR35.

#### Control test

Aldermoor's compliance requirements meant Tom needed to attend specific weekly meetings on their premises and work to hours largely set by their change-control process rather than his own schedule — a materially higher degree of control than the Northgate arrangement.

#### Substitution

No effective right of substitution was built into this engagement; Aldermoor's onboarding and security clearance process is specific to the named individual, which in practice rules out sending someone else.

#### Practical effect

Because the determination came back inside IR35 and we are the fee-payer for this engagement, we operate PAYE and employee NI on the days Tom bills against the Aldermoor project specifically, while continuing to pay Vickers Dev Ltd gross, without deductions, for his Northgate days under the separate outside-IR35 determination. Ellie runs these as two entirely separate payment runs to avoid the two getting tangled in the payroll software.

## CEST and the paper trail

We run every new associate engagement through HMRC's Check Employment Status for Tax (CEST) tool and keep the PDF output alongside a one-page Status Determination Statement in the contract folder for that engagement. This isn't just box-ticking — if HMRC ever queries a determination, "we used CEST and kept the output" is a meaningfully stronger position than "we made a judgement call," even though CEST's own outputs aren't binding on HMRC.

## Contract renewal reminders

Both SDSs are reviewed at contract renewal, not just at the start. Calendar reminder set for each renewal date; a status determination made in 2024 doesn't necessarily still hold if the working arrangement has drifted.

## Other associates

We've used two other freelance developers on a one-off basis (a weekend of emergency cover each) where the engagement was too short and too clearly outside any test of control to bother with a formal CEST run — HMRC's own guidance treats genuinely short, task-based engagements with no ongoing relationship differently, though we've noted internally that "too short to bother" is a judgement call we should tighten up into an actual policy threshold rather than deciding case by case.
