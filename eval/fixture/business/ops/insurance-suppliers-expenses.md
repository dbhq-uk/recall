# Insurance, Suppliers and Expenses

## Professional indemnity and cyber insurance

Our professional indemnity policy is with Hiscox — Hiscox Professional Indemnity, policy number HPI-3391847, £1,000,000 limit of indemnity — placed and renewed annually each 1 September through our broker, Cavendish Insurance Brokers. The 2025 renewal premium rose to £2,140, up from £1,890 the year before; Cavendish's read is that this reflects sector-wide rate hardening for tech consultancies generally rather than anything in our own claims history (we have none, touch wood).

We also hold a separate cyber liability policy, £250,000 limit, through the same broker, added in 2023 after a healthcare client's pre-contract security questionnaire made proof of cyber cover a precondition of signature. Same renewal date as the PI policy, bundled for a small discount.

### Why we bother with both

PI covers claims arising from our advice or work product being wrong or negligent; cyber covers the cost of a breach — notification, forensics, business interruption — regardless of whether the breach was our fault. A client asking "are you insured" is usually only thinking about the first one, but the second is the one that would actually sink us if we lost a laptop with the wrong data on it.

## Suppliers

### Accounting: Xero

We run Xero on the Grow plan, currently £36/month, which covers unlimited invoicing and bank reconciliation for the two of us (Ellie and our accountant) who touch it directly.

### Accountancy: Fernbank Accountants

Our external accountant is Naomi Reyes at Fernbank Accountants, who handles year-end statutory accounts, the Companies House confirmation statement, and corporation tax computations. Monthly management accounts are produced in-house by Ellie from the Xero data; Fernbank reviews quarterly.

### Hosting: AWS

AWS is our primary hosting supplier across client projects, on the Business Support plan (the mid tier, giving us sub-one-hour response on production-down tickets, which has mattered twice). Individual client architectures are documented in their own engagement notes rather than here.

### Laptops: Apple Business Manager + Bridgeworth Asset Finance

Standard issue is a MacBook Pro 14" with the M3 Pro chip, procured through Apple Business Manager and financed on a three-year lease through Bridgeworth Asset Finance rather than bought outright — keeps the kit refreshed on a predictable cycle without a lump capital outlay each time, and the lease payments are a clean monthly opex line rather than a depreciation schedule Fernbank has to think about.

### GitHub: Enterprise Cloud upgrade

We ran on GitHub Team for years — perfectly adequate for a five-person shop. In January 2026 we upgraded to GitHub Enterprise Cloud, at roughly five times the per-seat cost, specifically because the same healthcare client's security questionnaire required SAML single sign-on tied to our identity provider, which Team doesn't support. A blunt instrument for one client's requirement, but it's now also let us enforce SSO across every repo, which we'd been meaning to do anyway.

## Expense policy

### Mileage and subsistence

Mileage is claimed at the HMRC Approved Mileage Allowance Payment rate: 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a tax year, 25p per mile after that. Anyone working from home more than incidentally can claim the HMRC simplified flat-rate home working allowance of £6 per week without needing to itemise bills.

### Claims process

Everyone has a Pleo card for day-to-day business spend. Receipts are required for anything over £25 (Pleo's app makes this a photo, not a drawer of paper). Ellie reconciles on the 5th of each month; anything not categorised by then gets chased individually, and repeatedly missing the deadline is a standing joke that's edging toward not being a joke.

### What doesn't get reimbursed

Client entertainment above £40 a head needs sign-off from Alex first, not claimed-then-queried. This one exists because of a single dinner in 2024 that we won't be repeating the details of here.
