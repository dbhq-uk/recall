# Team Utilisation — 2026

Running notes on where everyone's days actually go, reconciled from the Xero
timesheets each month. Kept mostly because our instincts about who is busy turn
out to be wrong about a third of the time.

## Definitions

**Billable utilisation** is billable days divided by available days, where
available excludes holiday, bank holidays and sickness but *includes* internal
work. Internal work counts against you deliberately: a week spent on our own
website is a week nobody paid for, and calling it "non-available" would hide that.

Target is 65% for delivery staff. Not 100%, and not 80% — the gap is where
estimating, discovery calls, code review and the occasional afternoon of actually
thinking live, and squeezing it is how a consultancy quietly stops being any good.

## Tom Vickers — day split

Worth setting out separately because it trips people up, and because Ellie has to
process it as two entirely separate payment runs rather than one:

| Month | Northgate days | Aldermoor days | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | 9 | 7 | 16 |
| February | 8 | 8 | 16 |
| March | 7 | 9 | 16 |

The split is not an accident of scheduling. The two engagements are contracted,
billed and paid on completely different bases, and the reason for that is set out
in the IR35 notes — read those before touching anything to do with how Tom is
paid, and do not try to reason it out from this table, which records only how many
days went where.

Ellie's standing request: book Tom's time against the right project code on the
day, not at the end of the month. A misallocated day between these two projects is
not a rounding error, it is a payroll correction.

## Priya at 71%

Above target, and the third quarter running. This is the number that worries Sam
most on the whole page. She is the single specialist on more than one thing — the
Bramwell & Foss retro said this in as many words and we have not fixed it — and a
71% utilisation leaves no slack to absorb the moment something unusual lands.
Rob's hire has taken roughly a day a week of small work off her, which took her
from 78% to 71%, so the direction is right and the destination is not reached.

## Alex at 10%

Correct and intended. Alex sells, hires, and runs the company. The six billable
days in the quarter were two discovery workshops she ran herself because the
client specifically asked for her, which is a good problem and not a scalable one.

## What we do with this

Nothing automatic. It is a conversation starter at the monthly ops call, not a
target anyone is measured against individually — the moment utilisation becomes a
personal KPI, people start booking time creatively and the number stops meaning
anything at all.
